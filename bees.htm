Life in the Hive

A single hive is a whole working community. There is one queen, who lays the eggs, and thousands of worker bees, who are all female. They collect nectar and pollen, build comb, feed the young and guard the entrance. The male bees, called drones, have one job: mating with a queen.

Bees turn flower nectar into honey by drying it down inside the wax cells. When it is ready, they seal each cell with a thin wax cap. That capped honey is what the beekeeper harvests.

From Hive to Bottle

  1. Check the frames. Only frames that are mostly capped get pulled, so the honey is ripe.
  2. Uncap the comb. The thin wax caps are sliced off to open the cells.
  3. Spin it out. Frames go into an extractor that spins the honey out of the comb.
  4. Strain it. Honey runs through a strainer to catch wax bits.
  5. Bottle it. Honey pours out through a honey gate straight into the bottles.

Quick Bee Facts

  • Worker bees communicate where flowers are with a movement called the waggle dance.
  • A strong hive can hold tens of thousands of bees in the summer.
  • Beeswax is made by the bees themselves, from glands on their bodies.
  • Honey that is stored sealed can keep for a very long time.
